Why Choose Daito-ryu Jujutsu in Blackpool?

Craig Abernethy August 5, 2026 8 min read

If you've been searching for traditional Japanese jujutsu near me in the Blackpool area, you've likely come across Daito-ryu jujutsu. But what exactly is it, and why should you consider training in this centuries-old martial art?

Daito-ryu jujutsu (大東流柔術) is one of the oldest and most respected Japanese martial arts in existence. With roots stretching back over 900 years, it is the art from which many modern jujutsu and aikido styles descend. At Confluence Martial Arts in Thornton-Cleveleys, we teach authentic Daito-ryu jujutsu as it has been passed down through generations — practical, effective, and deeply rewarding.

A Brief History of Daito-ryu Jujutsu

Daito-ryu's origins trace back to the Minamoto clan in the 12th century, but it was systematised and formalised by Takeda Sokaku in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Takeda travelled across Japan teaching the art to samurai, police officers, and martial artists from all walks of life. His most famous student, Morihei Ueshiba, went on to found aikido, but the full Daito-ryu curriculum remained distinct — and far more comprehensive.

The art is preserved in the Hiden-mokuroku, a scroll containing the core techniques of the system. These techniques form the backbone of what students learn at Confluence Martial Arts today, adapted for safe, modern practice while retaining their original martial intent.

What You Will Learn

Daito-ryu jujutsu is a complete martial system. Unlike many modern martial arts that focus on one range of combat, Daito-ryu covers all the key ranges you would need for real self-defence:

Joint Locks (Kansetsu-waza)

The hallmark of Daito-ryu. You will learn to control an opponent's joints — wrists, elbows, shoulders — with precise, mechanical leverage. These techniques are not about strength; they are about angles, timing, and body mechanics. A smaller person can control a much larger attacker with proper technique.

Throws (Nage-waza)

Daito-ryu contains a rich vocabulary of throws and takedowns. Using an attacker's momentum and balance against them, you learn to put them on the ground safely and decisively. These are not sport judo throws — they are designed for self-defence scenarios where you need to end a confrontation quickly.

Strikes (Atemi-waza)

While Daito-ryu is primarily a grappling art, it includes strategic strikes to vulnerable points. These strikes are used to create openings for locks and throws, or to disable an attacker when necessary. You will learn where to strike, when, and why.

Groundwork (Ne-waza)

Real fights often end up on the ground. Daito-ryu includes practical ground-fighting techniques that focus on control, escape, and getting back to your feet safely.

Why Daito-ryu for Modern Self-Defence?

You might wonder whether a 900-year-old Japanese martial art is relevant to the streets of Blackpool in 2026. The answer is a resounding yes — and here is why.

Traditional martial arts like Daito-ryu were developed in an era when combat was a matter of life and death. The techniques were tested, refined, and passed down because they worked. Modern self-defence situations — whether that is dealing with an aggressive individual on the promenade or handling a confrontation in a car park — share the same fundamental dynamics: an attacker closing distance, grabbing, pushing, or striking.

Daito-ryu excels in exactly these scenarios. Its joint locks allow you to control an aggressor without excessive force. Its throws let you end a confrontation without throwing a single punch. And its strikes, when needed, target the most vulnerable areas of the body for maximum effect with minimum effort.

"Daito-ryu jujutsu is not about fighting — it is about resolving conflict through control and understanding of movement. The techniques work because they are based on universal principles of physics and anatomy, not on athleticism or brute strength."

— Craig Abernethy, Confluence Martial Arts

Who Can Train?

Daito-ryu jujutsu is for everyone. The techniques are designed to work regardless of your size, strength, or fitness level. We have students in their teens and students in their sixties. Some come with years of martial arts experience; others have never set foot in a dojo before. What they all share is a willingness to learn and a respect for the art.

You do not need to be flexible, fit, or young. You just need to show up with an open mind. The training will improve your fitness, flexibility, and confidence as a natural by-product — but none of these are prerequisites.
