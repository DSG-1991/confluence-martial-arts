Daito-ryu Jujutsu vs Other Martial Arts — What Makes Traditional Japanese Jujutsu Different?

Craig Abernethy August 5, 2026 10 min read
Daito-ryu jujutsu — a complete traditional martial system taught at Cleveleys Citizens Hall

If you are researching martial arts in the Blackpool and Fylde Coast area, you have probably come across a wide range of options — Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ), karate, judo, krav maga, and many others. Each has its strengths, and each attracts different people for different reasons. But if you are looking for something deeper — a martial art with centuries of history, a complete curriculum, and a focus on real-world effectiveness — you may be wondering how Daito-ryu jujutsu compares to other martial arts.

At Confluence Martial Arts in Thornton-Cleveleys, we teach authentic Daito-ryu jujutsu alongside Takeuchi Renshinkan. In this article, we will compare Daito-ryu with the most popular martial arts available today, so you can make an informed decision about which path is right for you.

What Makes Daito-ryu Jujutsu Unique?

Before we compare Daito-ryu to other arts, it is worth understanding what makes it distinct. Daito-ryu jujutsu (大東流柔術) is one of the oldest Japanese martial arts, with roots stretching back over 900 years. It is a comprehensive system that includes joint locks, throws, strikes, and ground fighting — all taught within a single, coherent curriculum based on the Hiden-mokuroku scroll.

Unlike many modern martial arts that specialise in one area of combat, Daito-ryu was designed for the realities of self-defence and battlefield survival. Its techniques are not constrained by sport rules, weight classes, or competition formats. They are designed to work in the messy, unpredictable world of real confrontation.

Daito-ryu Jujutsu vs Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ)

This is perhaps the most common comparison, as both arts are grappling-based. However, they approach grappling from fundamentally different perspectives.

BJJ: Sport Grappling with a Focus on Ground Fighting

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu is a modern martial art that evolved from Kodokan judo in the early 20th century. It has become enormously popular thanks to its effectiveness in mixed martial arts (MMA) and its well-developed sport competition scene. BJJ specialises in ground fighting — taking an opponent to the ground, establishing dominant positions, and applying joint locks or chokeholds to force a submission.

BJJ is excellent at what it does. Its competitive framework means techniques are pressure-tested against fully resisting opponents, and its ground game is arguably the most refined in the world. If your primary interest is sport grappling or MMA, BJJ is an excellent choice.

Daito-ryu: Standing Control and Versatility

Daito-ryu jujutsu takes a different approach. While it includes ground fighting, its primary focus is on standing techniques — joint locks, throws, and controls that can be applied while both parties are on their feet. This is because real self-defence situations rarely start on the ground. An attacker grabs you, pushes you, or swings at you while you are standing. Daito-ryu gives you the tools to handle that moment — to control the attacker's joints, break their balance, and end the confrontation without ever going to the ground.

Daito-ryu also does not rely on athleticism in the same way that BJJ does. Its techniques are based on leverage, angles, and timing — principles that work regardless of your size, strength, or fitness level. A smaller, older practitioner can apply Daito-ryu techniques effectively against a larger, stronger attacker, because the techniques exploit the natural weaknesses of the human body rather than requiring physical dominance.

Another key difference is that Daito-ryu includes strikes (atemi-waza) as an integral part of the system. In BJJ, striking is not part of the curriculum. In a real self-defence situation, the ability to strike — even as a distraction to set up a lock or throw — is invaluable.

Daito-ryu Jujutsu vs Karate

Karate: Striking Specialisation

Karate is primarily a striking art, focusing on punches, kicks, knee strikes, and open-hand techniques. It has a strong sporting component (kumite) and a rich tradition of kata (forms). Karate develops excellent striking skills, fitness, and discipline.

Daito-ryu: A Broader Curriculum

Where karate specialises in striking, Daito-ryu covers striking, grappling, throwing, and joint locking within a single system. A Daito-ryu practitioner learns to strike when appropriate, but also knows what to do when the opponent closes the distance — something that pure strikers often struggle with. Daito-ryu's atemi-waza (striking techniques) target vulnerable points of the body and are used strategically to create openings for locks and throws, rather than as the primary means of attack.

If you are looking for a striking-focused workout with a strong sporting element, karate is a great option. If you want a more complete martial education that prepares you for all ranges of combat, Daito-ryu offers a broader path.

Daito-ryu Jujutsu vs Judo

Judo: Sport Throwing and Groundwork

Judo, founded by Jigoro Kano in the late 19th century, is a modern martial art derived from classical jujutsu. It focuses on throws (nage-waza) and ground grappling (ne-waza), with a strong competitive framework. Judo is an Olympic sport and has a well-developed system of ranks and competitions.

Judo's throwing techniques are among the most refined in the world. If your goal is to learn how to throw an opponent to the ground with power and control, judo is exceptional.

Daito-ryu: Beyond the Throw

Daito-ryu also contains a rich vocabulary of throws, but they are taught in a different context. In Daito-ryu, a throw is not the end of the technique — it is a transition. You throw the opponent, but you maintain control through a joint lock as they fall, or you follow them to the ground to apply a finishing technique. The throw is part of a continuous flow of action, not a discrete scoring event.

Daito-ryu also includes techniques that judo has largely removed from its curriculum — particularly the more dangerous joint locks and strikes. Judo, as a sport, has rules to protect competitors. Daito-ryu, as a self-defence system, has no such restrictions. Its techniques are designed to end a confrontation, not to score points.

Daito-ryu Jujutsu vs Krav Maga

Krav Maga: Modern Military Self-Defence

Krav Maga is a modern self-defence system developed for the Israeli military. It is practical, aggressive, and designed to be learned quickly. It focuses on defending against common attacks — chokes, grabs, weapon threats — and uses simple, effective counter-attacks.

Krav Maga has the advantage of being relatively easy to learn in a short time. If you need basic self-defence skills quickly, it can be effective.

Daito-ryu: Depth and Long-Term Development

Daito-ryu takes a different approach. Rather than teaching a set of pre-programmed responses to specific attacks, it teaches principles of movement, balance, and control that can be applied to any situation. This takes longer to learn, but the understanding you develop is deeper and more adaptable.

Daito-ryu also offers something that Krav Maga does not — a rich cultural and historical tradition. Training in Daito-ryu connects you to a lineage that stretches back centuries. It is not just about learning to defend yourself; it is about participating in a living tradition of martial art.

Why Choose Traditional Japanese Jujutsu?

After comparing Daito-ryu with other popular martial arts, you may be wondering: why choose traditional Japanese jujutsu over more common styles? Here are the key reasons:

  • Completeness — Daito-ryu covers striking, throwing, joint locking, and ground fighting in one system. You do not need to cross-train in multiple arts to be well-rounded.
  • Efficiency of movement — Daito-ryu techniques are based on leverage and body mechanics, not strength. They work for people of all sizes and fitness levels.
  • Real-world focus — The techniques are designed for self-defence, not sport. There are no rules to restrict what you can do.
  • Depth — The Hiden-mokuroku curriculum provides a lifetime of learning. There is always more to discover, more nuance to explore.
  • Tradition — Training in Daito-ryu connects you to a martial lineage that stretches back centuries. You are not just learning techniques — you are preserving a living tradition.

"Every martial art has value. The question is not which art is 'best' — it is which art aligns with your goals, your values, and the kind of person you want to become. Daito-ryu jujutsu offers a path of depth, tradition, and genuine skill that is increasingly rare in the modern world."

— Craig Abernethy, Confluence Martial Arts
